# RBI gold-loan collateral rules (India, as of Oct 2026) and implications for coins vs bars vs jewellery

## What does the RBI direction actually allow as collateral (coins, bars/bullion, ornaments; 22k vs 24k)? Was there an April 2026 rule banning bars and allowing only bank-minted 22k coins?

### Takeaway
The final RBI directions (issued 6 Jun 2025, updated 29 Sep 2025, compliance deadline 1 Apr 2026) allow gold/silver jewellery, ornaments and coins; they bar loans against primary gold/silver (bars, bullion). The final text as fetched has a 50 g gold-coin cap per borrower but, unlike the April 2025 DRAFT, no "22 carat minimum / sold by banks only" coin condition. The Reddit claim is a conflation: the bar exclusion is real (and was in the 2025 directions, effective 1 Apr 2026), but "only bank-minted 22k coins" comes from the draft and from secondary blogs; I could not confirm it in the final text.

### Cited Findings
- Final Directions: "Lending Against Gold and Silver Collateral) Directions, 2025", issued 6 Jun 2025, updated 29 Sep 2025; applies to commercial banks, co-op banks, NBFCs incl. HFCs; compliance "no later than April 1, 2026" (para 4) — [RBI notification](https://rbi.org.in/scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Mode=0&Id=12859)
- Eligible Collateral (para 6) = "jewellery, ornaments or coins made of gold or silver" — [RBI](https://rbi.org.in/scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Mode=0&Id=12859)
- Para 12: lender shall not lend (a) to purchase gold in any form (primary gold, ornaments, jewellery, coins) or gold-backed financial assets (ETFs, mutual fund units), and (b) against primary gold or silver. Para 12 was amended 29 Sep 2025 (DOR.CRE.REC.52/21.01.023/2025-26) — [RBI](https://rbi.org.in/scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Mode=0&Id=12859)
- Narrow exception: commercial banks / tier 3-4 UCBs may give need-based working-capital finance against primary gold/silver only to borrowers using it as an industrial-processing input (not retail) — [Cyril Amarchand Mangaldas](https://corporate.cyrilamarchandblogs.com/2025/11/fig-paper-no-52-rbi-directions-on-lending-against-gold-and-silver-collateral-a-harmonised-regulatory-framework/)
- Para 16 caps: coins, 50 g gold / 500 g silver aggregate per borrower across all loans; ornaments, 1 kg gold / 10 kg silver per borrower — [RBI](https://rbi.org.in/scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Mode=0&Id=12859); [CAM](https://corporate.cyrilamarchandblogs.com/2025/11/fig-paper-no-52-rbi-directions-on-lending-against-gold-and-silver-collateral-a-harmonised-regulatory-framework/)
- Final text valuation: "reference price corresponding to its actual purity (caratage)"; only intrinsic gold value counted, no stones/gems (paras 17-18) — [RBI](https://rbi.org.in/scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Mode=0&Id=12859); [abcaus mirror](https://abcaus.in/rbi/rbi-lending-against-gold-silver-collateral-directions-2025.html)
- Three separate fetches of the final text/commentary found no coin purity minimum, no bank-sold requirement, no mandatory 22k valuation basis — [RBI](https://rbi.org.in/scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Mode=0&Id=12859); [abcaus](https://abcaus.in/rbi/rbi-lending-against-gold-silver-collateral-directions-2025.html); [CAM](https://corporate.cyrilamarchandblogs.com/2025/11/fig-paper-no-52-rbi-directions-on-lending-against-gold-and-silver-collateral-a-harmonised-regulatory-framework/)
- The April 2025 DRAFT did say: "specially minted gold coins, with a purity of 22 carats or higher, sold by banks"; coins sold by non-banks not "specified coins"; 50 g cap; valuation on 22-carat price with proportionate adjustment for lower purity — [RBI draft](https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/bs_viewcontent.aspx?Id=4633)
- Many lender/fintech blogs repeat "22-carat bank-minted coins, 50 g, 22k valuation benchmark" as the effective-1-Apr-2026 rule — e.g. [Upstox](https://upstox.com/learning-center/personal-finance/rbi-gold-loan-rules-2026/article-1545/), [Airtel](https://www.airtel.in/blog/gold-loan/rbi-gold-loan-policy-changes-what-it-means-for-you/); this conflicts with the final-text readings above (likely draft carry-over).
- Searches found no separate 2026 RBI amendment/circular on gold collateral; effective date 1 Apr 2026 is the compliance deadline of the 2025 directions, applying to fresh loans (existing loans continue under old framework) — [CAM](https://corporate.cyrilamarchandblogs.com/2025/11/fig-paper-no-52-rbi-directions-on-lending-against-gold-and-silver-collateral-a-harmonised-regulatory-framework/)

### Inferences
- Bars/bullion/biscuits cannot be pledged with any regulated lender since 1 Apr 2026 (the Reddit commenter is right on that part). 24k vs 22k is irrelevant to eligibility in the final text; purity only affects valuation (24k coin is valued at its actual purity).
- Practically, individual lenders may still impose own restrictions (bank-sold coins, hallmarked, 22k) even if RBI's final text does not; verify per lender.

### Gaps
- Fetches were via a summarising tool, not raw text; I did not read the exact clause wording for coin purity myself. Recommend the writer re-check paras 6, 16, 17 in the RBI PDF and the 29 Sep 2025 amendment content (what exactly it changed in para 12 was not retrievable).
- No confirmation of any April 2026 RBI circular specifically; none found.

## LTV caps, ownership documentation, valuation, and lender practice (Muthoot, Manappuram, IIFL, Chola)

### Takeaway
Consumption-loan LTV caps are 85% (up to Rs 2.5 lakh), 80% (Rs 2.5-5 lakh), 75% (above Rs 5 lakh). Ownership declaration is required. Valuation uses the lower of 30-day average or previous-day closing price (IBJA/SEBI-regulated exchanges). Lender-specific coin practice: little reliable data found.

### Cited Findings
- LTV tiers 85/80/75% (para 19) — [RBI](https://rbi.org.in/scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Mode=0&Id=12859)
- Ownership: borrower document or declaration of rightful ownership (para 13) — [RBI](https://rbi.org.in/scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Mode=0&Id=12859)
- Valuation: lower of 30-day average closing price for that purity or previous-day closing price, IBJA or SEBI-regulated exchange (para 17) — [RBI](https://rbi.org.in/scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Mode=0&Id=12859)
- Bullet consumption loans capped at 12 months, renewable (para 15); credit assessment above Rs 2.5 lakh; gold returned within 7 working days or Rs 5,000/day penalty (secondary source) — [RBI](https://rbi.org.in/scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Mode=0&Id=12859); [Airtel blog summary](https://www.airtel.in/blog/gold-loan/rbi-gold-loan-policy-changes-what-it-means-for-you/)
- Muthoot is reported to accept gold coins and jewellery (aggregator source, undated) — [Paisabazaar/search snippet](https://www.paisabazaar.com/gold-loan/four-leading-non-banking-financial-companies-you-should-consider-for-a-gold-loan/); IIFL: ornaments of 18k or above (snippet, possibly pre-2026) — [Airtel list](https://www.airtel.in/blog/gold-loan/top-gold-loan-companies-in-india-2025-for-maximum-value/)

### Inferences
- Jewellery gets the loan with the least friction; coins are accepted but capped at 50 g (about Rs 6-8 lakh at 2026 prices is NOT reachable; 50 g is a small ticket).

### Gaps
- No primary source found for Manappuram, Chola, IIFL, SBI/HDFC coin policies post-April 2026. Check each lender's site.

## Alternatives: Gold Monetisation Scheme, SGB, gold ETFs

### Takeaway
RBI directions ban loans to buy gold ETFs/MF units and (per secondary sources) lending against primary gold and gold-backed financial assets; SGB/ETF-backed loans are not part of this gold-collateral framework and I found no confirmation of any lender regime for them.

### Cited Findings
- Para 12 bans loans for purchase of gold-backed ETFs/MF units and against primary gold/silver — [RBI](https://rbi.org.in/scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Mode=0&Id=12859); secondary sources say ETFs not eligible as collateral — [Upstox](https://upstox.com/learning-center/personal-finance/rbi-gold-loan-rules-2026/article-1545/)
- The final text fetched contains no reference to the Gold Monetisation Scheme — [RBI](https://rbi.org.in/scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Mode=0&Id=12859)

### Inferences
- GMS is a deposit route (deposit gold, earn interest), not a loan alternative; bars/coins can be deposited there in principle, but its current status (I recall the government discontinuing medium/long-term deposits in 2025) is unverified.

### Gaps
- GMS current status, SGB-backed loans (SGBs are accepted as collateral by banks under other norms, unverified), ETF-pledge loans: not researched to primary sources.

## Practical conclusion: which form to hold if a gold loan might be wanted

### Takeaway
Jewellery (hallmarked, 22k) is the only form with no weight issue (1 kg cap) for loans; coins qualify only up to 50 g per borrower; bars/bullion qualify for no regulated lender.

### Cited Findings
- Caps and bar exclusion as above — [RBI](https://rbi.org.in/scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Mode=0&Id=12859)

### Inferences
- If a loan is a realistic need: hold jewellery, or coins up to 50 g (preferably bank-sold, 22k+ hallmarked to satisfy stricter lender policy/draft criteria); bars/biscuits above that give no borrowing access. Stones/making charges are not counted in loan value, so jewellery's loan value is only its gold content.
- 22k vs 24k: purity does not block eligibility under final text; valuation is by actual purity.

### Gaps
- Lender-by-lender coin acceptance unverified.
