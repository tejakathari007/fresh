# Converting gold coins/bars into jewellery in India: routes, costs, and value lost (as of Oct 2026)

Research limits: several primary pages returned 403/404/503 to the fetch tool (Tanishq exchange T&C page, Malabar exchange-policy page, West Bengal GST FAQ PDF, Kalyan promise page). Brand policy details below therefore come from search-result snippets and secondary summaries unless stated. Worked examples are my own arithmetic on stated assumptions and are not sourced figures.

## 1. Routes and brand policies (Tanishq, Malabar, Kalyan, Joyalukkas, Bhima, Senco, local jewellers)

### Takeaway
There are three routes: (a) exchange at a brand store, (b) custom-make at a jeweller or goldsmith from your own gold, and (c) sell the bullion and buy jewellery. Large chains generally accept coins after a purity test, but deductions vary by brand and by coin origin, and the policies are not uniform. I found no official statement that 24k bullion is charged an alloying fee or extra gold. Published 24k and 22k treatment is mostly a purity-based valuation.

### Cited Findings
- Tanishq's exchange terms say 24kt, 22kt and 18kt plain gold jewellery or gold coins bought from jewellers other than Tanishq/Mia/Zoya are accepted at Tanishq stores. Studded items are not eligible. — [Tanishq exchange T&C (via search snippet)](https://www.tanishq.co.in/exchange-terms-and-conditions.html)
- Tanishq values items after a Karatmeter purity test and the valuation is "final and binding". Exchange is in-store only, not online. For its own jewellery, making charges, taxes and discounts on the original invoice are deducted in full. — [Tanishq exchange T&C (snippet)](https://www.tanishq.co.in/exchange-terms-and-conditions.html)
- A secondary comparison says Tanishq pays 100% of net gold weight (only making charges deducted), accepts other brands' jewellery and accepts coins/bars. — [Gullak, Gold Exchange Schemes in India 2025](https://gullak.money/blog/gold-exchange-2025/)
- Malabar advertises a zero-deduction exchange for its own jewellery (full gold weight and purity value). Outside jewellery gets a purity check and a net value. Within 14 days of delivery, unused jewellery can be exchanged without deducting making charges, but gold coins and customised/personalised orders are excluded from that window. After 14 days, the store's prevailing gold rate applies. — [Malabar exchange policy (snippet; the page is global and I could not confirm it is India-specific)](https://www.malabargoldanddiamonds.com/exchange-policy.html)
- Joyalukkas: one secondary source says its offers exclude coins and bars, with waived making charges and about Rs 100 extra per gram during promotions. Another snippet claims a 4% handling deduction on plain gold and 0% on coins, but that attribution looks muddled with CaratLane. I treat it as unreliable. — [Gullak](https://gullak.money/blog/gold-exchange-2025/); the Joyalukkas page found was the US site, not India — [Joyalukkas US](https://www.joyalukkas.com/us/exchange-return)
- Kalyan (anecdotal, social media, around Jun 2026): a campaign bought back other brands' coins/bars at a 3% deduction versus a usual 6%. Reported own-brand coin deduction is about 2%. A reported 22k deduction is 5%. Coins are machine-tested, and purity below the stamped level (e.g. under 995) can attract extra deductions. This is a promotional claim and not official policy. — [X post by Gold Deals](https://x.com/GoldDealsIndia/status/2060991550738300995) and the search-result summary
- Senco: gold bars are said to be sellable back at market rate less a small charge. Its blog says coins can often be exchanged for jewellery once purity and authenticity are verified. It lists deductions for stones, enamel, soldering, impurities, melting/refining, and does not give percentages. It advises asking for purity testing in your presence and carrying the original bill and ID, and says shops may accept old gold without a bill. — [Senco blog](https://sencogoldanddiamonds.com/blog/old-gold-exchange-how-to-get-best-value)
- Bhima: a promotional item says 100% value on exchange of old 22kt jewellery (Akshaya Tritiya). Nothing found on coins or bars. — [Gulf News (UAE promo, may not apply in India)](https://gulfnews.com/gn-focus/exclusive-akshaya-tritiya-offers-at-bhima-jewellers-1.1714740672445)
- Purity testing methods in use are Karatmeter and XRF (non-destructive) and acid test. — [IIFL](https://www.iifl.com/blogs/gold-loan/gold-jewellery-exchange-offer-at-jewellers)
- Hallmarked pieces generally do not attract wastage deductions, because purity is pre-certified. — [IIFL](https://www.iifl.com/blogs/gold-loan/gold-jewellery-exchange-offer-at-jewellers)

### Inferences
- Route (b) custom-make avoids paying 3% GST a second time on the gold. Route (a) usually means the jeweller buys your coin and sells you new jewellery, so GST of 3% applies to the full new jewellery price. This is inferred from the GST rules below, not stated by any brand.
- Converting 24k to 22k requires adding about 8.4% alloy by weight (999 to 916). Alloy is cheap, so 10g of 24k becomes about 10.9g of 22k. I found no source charging for it. The risk is that a jeweller bills labour on the larger final weight. Ask whether labour is charged on input weight or output weight.
- A 22k coin avoids the alloy step and is simpler to exchange, but 22k coins are less liquid and are not priced identically across brands.

### Gaps
- I found no official, verifiable India-page text for Kalyan, Joyalukkas, Bhima or Senco on own versus other-brand coin deductions.
- I found no source on 24k-specific alloying fees.
- Local jewellers' practices rest on no published policy, so I treated them as negotiable.
- I could not retrieve the Tanishq or Malabar full T&C pages, so check the current text in store.

## 2. Making charges and wastage

### Takeaway
Making charges on ready-made jewellery are typically 5-25% of gold value (plain machine-made chains at the low end, wedding and handmade pieces above 20%), plus wastage of 5-7% sometimes billed separately. Coins carry roughly 1-5%. When you supply gold, the jeweller usually charges labour and wastage per gram or as a percentage, and I found no authoritative published rule on the basis.

### Cited Findings
- Making charges on 22k typically span 5-25% of value depending on design complexity and jeweller reputation. — [Goodreturns](https://www.goodreturns.in/classroom/gold-jewellery-making-charges-in-india-011-1462569.html)
- Machine-made mass-market jewellery such as simple chains is 3-25%. Intricate and wedding jewellery can exceed 25%. Studded pieces cost more. — [BankBazaar](https://www.bankbazaar.com/gold-rate/making-and-wastage-charges-gold-jewellery.html)
- Wastage is typically 5-7% and can exceed 10% for intricate or handmade designs. Charges can be per gram (fixed) or a percentage. — [Goodreturns](https://www.goodreturns.in/classroom/gold-jewellery-making-charges-in-india-011-1462569.html)
- Coin making charges are about 3-5% (another source says 1-4%). Jewellery cutting, filing and soldering lose 2-5% of the gold worked, which is billed back to the buyer. — [IIFL via search summary](https://www.iifl.com/blogs/gold-loan/making-charges-on-gold-coin-and-biscuit-why-they-are-low)
- Making charges, wastage and discounts are negotiable and promotional (for example 25% off making at Malabar festive offers; waived making at Joyalukkas). — [Gullak](https://gullak.money/blog/gold-exchange-2025/)

### Inferences
- For modelling I use these making plus wastage ranges: low 8% (plain machine chain on promotion), mid 12%, high 20% (handmade necklace or temple piece).
- For custom-make from supplied gold, I model labour at 6% / 10% / 15% on the gold weight, lower than retail making because there is no retail margin. This is an assumption and needs a local quote.

### Gaps
- No source gave temple-jewellery-specific percentages.
- No authoritative source says whether making is charged on full weight when you supply gold.

## 3. Melting and purity deductions

### Takeaway
Expect 1-3% refining/melting charges plus possible purity-based deductions, higher for non-hallmarked or doubtful items. Coins and bars from known mints are valued by Karatmeter/XRF with small deductions.

### Cited Findings
- Refining/melting charges typically run 1-3% of gold value. — [IIFL](https://www.iifl.com/blogs/gold-loan/gold-jewellery-exchange-offer-at-jewellers)
- Purity table: 24k = 0.999, 22k = 0.916, 18k = 0.750. — [IIFL](https://www.iifl.com/blogs/gold-loan/gold-jewellery-exchange-offer-at-jewellers)
- Common deductions are stones, enamel, impurities, solder, melting/refining. — [Senco](https://sencogoldanddiamonds.com/blog/old-gold-exchange-how-to-get-best-value)
- Casting, filing and polishing lose 2-5% of gold worked (see section 2). — [IIFL summary](https://www.iifl.com/blogs/gold-loan/making-charges-on-gold-coin-and-biscuit-why-they-are-low)

### Inferences
- I model melt/handling loss of 1% / 2% / 5% (low/mid/high) on the custom route. The 5% case assumes an unbranded or untested bar at a local jeweller.

### Gaps
- No published testing-fee schedules were found.

## 4. GST

### Takeaway
Ready-made jewellery is taxed at 3% on the whole value, including making. When you supply your own gold, the job work is a service taxed at 5% on labour only, and the gold is not taxed again. Exchange at a store is not clearly a margin-scheme case, so assume 3% on the full new price.

### Cited Findings
- Where a customer supplies gold to a goldsmith, GST is 5% on the job-work charges and not 3%, since making is the main supply. — [Legal Suvidha / search summary of CBIC and state FAQs](https://legalsuvidha.com/blog/gst-on-gold-jewellery-india-2025); the West Bengal FAQ page is the likely primary source but was unavailable — [WB Commercial Taxes FAQ](https://comtax.wb.gov.in/GST/GST_FAQ/FAQ_on_Gems_&_Jewellery_under_GST.pdf)
- Ready-made jewellery (composite supply) is taxed at 3% on gold plus making. Custom jewellery with separately itemised labour may carry 3% on gold and 5% on making. Making uses SAC 9988. — [PKC India](https://www.pkcindia.com/blog/gst-on-gold-2026-rate-on-jewellery-making-charges-coins-digital-gold-explained/)
- Old-gold exchange can fall under the margin scheme (Rule 32(5) CGST Rules), taxing only the margin, but only if the gold was bought from an unregistered person and sold without change in form and without ITC. Conflicting AAR rulings exist, including a Kerala ruling that gold is never second-hand. The safest position is that it applies only if the gold is not melted or recast. — [PKC India](https://www.pkcindia.com/blog/gst-on-gold-2026-rate-on-jewellery-making-charges-coins-digital-gold-explained/)

### Inferences
- Since coin-to-jewellery involves melting and a change of form, the margin scheme probably does not apply, so route (a) likely means 3% GST on the full new jewellery value in addition to the 3% you paid on the coin. Confirm this on the invoice.
- I could not verify this against a CBIC circular text. Treat it as unverified.

### Gaps
- No CBIC circular or notification text was retrieved directly. The 3% / 5% rates are widely reported but confirmed only through secondary sources here.

## 5. Worked example (illustrative, my own arithmetic)

Method: per 100 of fine-gold value at spot on the day of purchase, and assuming no gold price move so only frictions show. 24k and 22k bullion behave the same per fine gram. A 22k coin has a similar structure but lower alloy needs. Figures are outlay needed per 100 of gold value ending up in the jewellery.

Assumptions (low / mid / high):
- Bullion premium or coin making: 1% / 3% / 6%, plus 3% GST.
- Direct jewellery making plus wastage: 8% / 12% / 20%, plus 3% GST.
- Exchange deduction on coin: 0% / 2% / 5%.
- Custom-make melt loss: 1% / 2% / 5%. Labour: 6% / 10% / 15%, plus 5% GST on labour.
- Sale deduction on bullion: 2% / 4% / 6%.

| Case | Direct jewellery | (a) Brand exchange | (b) Custom-make | (c) Sell and buy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Low | 111.2 | 115.3 | 111.3 | 117.2 |
| Mid | 115.4 | 123.5 | 118.8 | 125.5 |
| High | 123.6 | 137.8 | 130.7 | 138.8 |

Excess over buying jewellery directly, points per 100 of gold: (a) +4 / +8 / +14, (b) about 0 / +3 / +7, (c) +6 / +10 / +15. Part of the gap in (a) and (c) is paying 3% GST twice on gold and the exchange or sale deduction. In (b) the saving comes from 5% GST on labour only.

Reading: the bullion plan costs about 3-15% more than buying jewellery directly, because you pay the bullion premium and GST first and the conversion costs second. It wins only if the jewellery wouldn't otherwise be bought, or if the jeweller waives making on exchange (promotions) or you choose a plain, low-labour design. Direct jewellery also carries a resale haircut later, not modelled here. Resale of jewellery at the same brand is typically 0-5% off gold value after making is lost.

Per-weight note: 10g of 24k bar gives about 10.9g of 22k at the same fine gold. A 10g 22k piece contains about 9.17g fine gold, so you only need about 9.2g of 24k bullion.

## 6. Practical tips

### Cited Findings
- Carry the original invoice, any certificate, and ID. Ask for purity testing in your presence. — [Senco blog](https://sencogoldanddiamonds.com/blog/old-gold-exchange-how-to-get-best-value)
- Valuation by Karatmeter is final and binding at Tanishq, so ask for a written valuation before committing. — [Tanishq exchange T&C](https://www.tanishq.co.in/exchange-terms-and-conditions.html)
- Malabar's 14-day window excludes coins and the post-14-day rate is the day's store rate. — [Malabar](https://www.malabargoldanddiamonds.com/exchange-policy.html)

### Inferences
- Buy from the brand you plan to exchange with, because own-brand coins usually get the lowest deduction (for example about 2% versus 3-6% at Kalyan, anecdotal).
- Get in writing: deduction percentage, whether making is charged on input or output weight, whether GST is 3% on full value or 5% on labour, and any promotion-period making waiver.
- Compare route (b) quotes from at least two local jewellers and insist on a hallmarked (HUID) piece and an itemised bill.
