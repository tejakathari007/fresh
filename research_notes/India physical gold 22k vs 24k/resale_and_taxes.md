# India physical gold: resale/buyback losses and taxation (as of Oct 2026)

Coverage note: about 14 searches/fetches. Several primary pages (MMTC-PAMP blog, Tanishq, Malabar exchange page) returned 403/404, so brand policy claims rest on search-engine summaries, which are weak. Verify at the counter. Illustrative calculations are my own arithmetic and are labelled as such.

## 1. Buyback policies and typical deductions (brands, banks, dealers)

### Takeaway
Own-brand 24k coins/bars from the big names are typically bought back at roughly 2-3% below the day's rate (Kalyan reported 2% own brand, 3% others during a promo, "usual 6%" for other brands). MMTC-PAMP claims no deduction on its own products. Banks do not buy back. Tanishq/Malabar/Joyalukkas mostly offer exchange against new purchases rather than cash. Realistic round-trip friction is 3-8% including the buy premium, plus the 3% GST, which is not refunded.

### Cited Findings
- MMTC-PAMP states a buyback guarantee on its coins/bars via authorised dealers with "no hidden fees or price cuts", paying the day's buyback rate by same-day bank transfer; valuation is based on purity and recoverable weight — [MMTC-PAMP blog via search summary](https://www.mmtcpamp.com/blog/blog-detail/common-myths-about-selling-gold) (page itself returned 403; unverified directly). "No deduction" is a marketing claim; the buyback rate is itself below the sell rate.
- Banks (SBI, HDFC, Axis, Union Bank etc.) sell 24k coins (typically MMTC-PAMP/ MMTC Indian Gold Coin) but do not buy back; the RBI does not allow it, and SBI/HDFC requests for permission were not approved in the sources found — [Goodreturns](https://www.goodreturns.in/personal-finance/planning/3-reasons-to-avoid-buying-gold-coins-from-banks-1164911.html), [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/article/pf/sbi-hdfc-seek-rbi-nod-to-buy-gold-from-retail-investors-109052700082_1.html). Both are old articles; I found no evidence of change.
- Tanishq: exchange programme gives store credit against purchases and is "not a gold buyback, resale, or cash purchase programme"; accepts coins/bars (limits apply); typical deduction cited is 2-4% melting/refining charge, 2% for 22k per one user report — [Tanishq exchange page via search](https://www.tanishq.com/gold-exchange-program.html), [DigiRake guide](https://knowledge.digirake.com/lifestyle/tanishq-gold-exchange-policy). Low-quality sources for the percentages.
- Malabar: 100% of benchmark rate for coins exchanged after 14 days of purchase; coins, bars and silver articles are excluded from the "exchange within 14 days" policy; zero-deduction exchange applies to own jewellery — [Malabar exchange policy via search](https://www.malabargoldanddiamonds.com/exchange-policy.html) (direct fetch 404), [Gullak](https://gullak.money/blog/gold-exchange-2025/). Exact coin buyback terms not verified.
- Joyalukkas: advertises zero-deduction exchange and says jewellery from any jeweller can be sold/exchanged — [Gulf News ad content](https://gulfnews.com/business/corporate-news/festive-cashback-offer-from-joyalukkas-to-celebrate-this-diwali-1.1635877561794). Marketing, not terms.
- Kalyan: a deals account reported a campaign buying other brands' coins/bars at 3% deduction "instead of the usual 6%"; own-brand 24k coin about 2%; general rule of thumb jeweller buyback is about 3% below sell rate for own coin, 5% for outside make — [GoldDealsIndia on X](https://x.com/GoldDealsIndia/status/2060991550738300995) (social media, unverified, promotional).
- Local dealers: no India-specific credible source found. A US-centric result says local buyers pay 65-95% of melt; do not use for India.
- Bhima: no policy found.

### Inferences
- Buyback is quoted on purity-adjusted metal value (24k = 99.9/99.5%; 22k = 91.6%). 22k coins are less standard, often carry making charges, and are usually only exchangeable at the issuing jeweller, so 22k resale loses the making charge (commonly 3-12% on jewellery) in addition to the spread. 24k bars/coins from MMTC-PAMP/refiners have the lowest spread and the widest buyer pool. This is inference; no source gave a 22k vs 24k buyback comparison.
- GST (3%) paid on purchase is not refunded on buyback in any source found; the effective buyer's loss includes it.
- Sealed packaging/invoice/certificate requirements: not confirmed from primary sources. Treat the original invoice and intact assay card as required for brand buyback and for best price.

### Gaps
- Primary-source buyback percentages for MMTC-PAMP, Tanishq, Malabar, Kalyan, Joyalukkas, Bhima; bid-ask vs IBJA data; local dealer discounts in India.

## 2. Selling without a bill, cash rules, TCS/TDS, PAN/KYC

### Takeaway
Cash receipts of Rs 2 lakh or more per person/day/transaction/event are barred by s.269ST (penalty equal to the amount, s.271DA). Cash-linked 1% TCS on bullion above Rs 2 lakh is the commonly cited rule but sources conflict on jewellery thresholds. TDS/TCS at 0.1% (50 lakh) matters only to business-scale trades. Selling without a bill does not make gains tax free.

### Cited Findings
- Section 269ST prohibits receiving Rs 2,00,000 or more in cash in aggregate from one person in a day, for one transaction or event; penalty under s.271DA equals the cash amount — [Attica Gold on 269ST](https://www.atticagoldcompany.com/blogs/section-269st-gold-cash-limit/) (secondary).
- TCS: 1% collected by seller on cash receipts above Rs 2 lakh for bullion; one source says jewellery threshold is Rs 5 lakh and that bullion excludes coins/articles of 10 g or less; TCS applies only to the cash component — [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/income-tax/tcs-transactions-jewellery-bullion-coal-lignite-iron-ore.html), [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/article/pti-stories/tcs-to-be-levied-above-rs-2-lakh-only-if-payment-in-cash-cbdt-116062700651_1.html). Conflicts with [TaxGuru 2017](https://taxguru.in/income-tax/tcs-on-jewellery-sale-removed-or-kept-with-threshold-of-rs-2-lakhs.html), where the Finance Bill 2017 memorandum said it intended to omit jewellery TCS in view of s.269ST; I could not confirm the final law. Note these are old sources.
- From 1 April 2026 the Income-tax Act 2025 replaces the 1961 Act; TCS provisions consolidate in s.394 (old s.206C) — [ClearTax s.394](https://cleartax.in/s/section-394-income-tax-act-2025), [TaxGuru TCS chart 2026-27](https://taxguru.in/income-tax/tcs-rate-chart-tax-year-2026-27-income-tax-act-2025.html). I did not open these to confirm the bullion/jewellery rows.
- TCS 0.1% (old s.206C(1H)) above Rs 50 lakh receipts and buyer TDS 0.1% (s.194Q) above Rs 50 lakh purchases with Rs 10 crore turnover — business-scale only — [OroPocket](https://blog.oropocket.com/taxes-on-gold-and-silver-investments-in-india-fy-2026-27-gst-capital-gains-tds/). Caution: the same page misstates holding period (36 months/20% indexation) — see section 3; use with care.
- Taxgarden: without proof of cost, gains remain taxable; for pre-1 April 2001 gold, FMV on 1 April 2001 with a registered valuer's report can be used — [TaxGarden](https://taxgarden.in/blog/income-tax-on-sale-of-gold-jewellery-india-ay-2026-27).

### Inferences
- An unbilled sale to a local dealer usually means a lower price and no paper trail for cost basis; the seller can still report the gain, but without invoice the cost is hard to prove. Sales above Rs 2 lakh must go by bank transfer/cheque/UPI to avoid the 269ST problem for the dealer.
- PAN: mandatory for cash purchases of jewellery/bullion above Rs 2 lakh (jeweller's Rule 114B); not confirmed from a primary source in this research.

### Gaps
- Final status of TCS on jewellery (Rs 2 lakh vs 5 lakh vs omitted) under 1961 Act and the 2025 Act; PAN/KYC and PMLA reporting thresholds for dealers (Rs 10 lakh cash reporting for dealers in precious metals) not found.

## 3. Capital gains tax on physical gold (FY2026-27) and exchange treatment

### Takeaway
Physical gold held more than 24 months: LTCG 12.5% flat, no indexation, plus surcharge and 4% cess. Held 24 months or less: slab rate. Budget 2026 (1 Feb 2026) left the 12.5% rate unchanged; its gold change affected SGBs only.

### Cited Findings
- Long-term threshold 24 months (cut from 36 for transfers on or after 23 July 2024); LTCG 12.5% under s.112 with no indexation; STCG at slab — [TaxGarden](https://taxgarden.in/blog/income-tax-on-sale-of-gold-jewellery-india-ay-2026-27), [Business Standard on Budget 2024](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/news/budget-2024-hikes-ltcg-tax-rate-to-12-5-stcg-to-20-stt-on-f-o-also-up-124072300553_1.html).
- Contradicted: OroPocket lists physical bullion as 36 months and 20% with indexation, which is the pre-July-2024 regime and appears outdated — [OroPocket](https://blog.oropocket.com/taxes-on-gold-and-silver-investments-in-india-fy-2026-27-gst-capital-gains-tds/).
- Union Budget 2026: LTCG 12.5% unchanged; SGB exemption at maturity limited to original-issue subscribers — [Angel One](https://www.angelone.in/news/taxation/union-budget-2026-ltcg-tax-unchanged-at-12-5-stt-on-commodity-futures-hiked), [Value Research](https://www.valueresearchonline.com/learn/mutual-funds/sovereign-gold-bonds-budget-2026-tax-rule-change/). STT on commodity futures raised (not relevant to physical).
- Exchanging old gold at a jeweller is treated as a sale at the credited value, with capital gains on credited value minus cost; exchange is a "transfer" under s.2(47) — [TaxGarden](https://taxgarden.in/blog/income-tax-on-sale-of-gold-jewellery-india-ay-2026-27), [Finnovate](https://www.finnovate.in/learn/blog/taxation-of-gold-in-india-explained). Secondary sources; no court authority cited.
- Inherited gold: cost is previous owner's cost; holding period includes previous owner's — [TaxGarden](https://taxgarden.in/blog/income-tax-on-sale-of-gold-jewellery-india-ay-2026-27).
- s.54F exemption available if net consideration reinvested in a residential house (cap Rs 10 crore) — same source.
- GST: 3% on gold metal value, 5% on separately billed making charges — [OroPocket](https://blog.oropocket.com/taxes-on-gold-and-silver-investments-in-india-fy-2026-27-gst-capital-gains-tds/).

### Inferences
- Surcharge (10-25% on tax above income thresholds; LTCG surcharge on s.112 gains capped at 15%) and 4% cess add roughly 4-18% on top of 12.5% (effective about 13%-14.4%+). Surcharge details are from my background knowledge, not a fetched source; verify.
- Cost of acquisition should include the GST and premium paid (total invoice), which my worked example assumes.

### Gaps
- Official CBDT/Finance Act text for Income-tax Act 2025 section numbers on capital gains; any case law on bar-for-jewellery exchange.

## 4. Wealth disclosure and search/seizure limits

### Takeaway
Schedule AL applies to individuals/HUFs with total income above Rs 1 crore (ITR-2/3 only), reports bullion and jewellery at cost-based values as of 31 March. The 500/250/100 g limits are a 1994 CBDT seizure guideline, not a legal holding cap.

### Cited Findings
- Schedule AL is required when total income exceeds Rs 1 crore; includes jewellery and bullion, valued as at 31 March — [Upstox](https://upstox.com/news/personal-finance/tax/schedule-al-income-above-1-crore-brings-an-extra-itr-task-here-s-how-to-get-it-right-for-ay-2026-27/article-196428/), [Outlook Money](https://www.outlookmoney.com/tax/assets-and-liabilities-have-to-be-declared-in-itr-for-annual-income-exceeding-rs-1-crore). Note: threshold is on income, not assets; one source says values are at 31 March while the form instructs cost-based valuation — confirm in the ITR instructions.
- CBDT Instruction 1916 (11 May 1994): for persons not assessed to wealth tax, jewellery up to 500 g per married woman, 250 g per unmarried woman, 100 g per male family member need not be seized; officers may exclude more considering family status and custom; courts treat this quantity as explained — [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/income-tax/analysis-cbdt-instruction-seizure-jewellery.html), [Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/gold-seizure-during-income-tax-search).

### Inferences
- The limits apply to jewellery and ornaments; coins/bars are not clearly covered, so investment bars above these limits need invoices as proof of source.

### Gaps
- Confirmation of any 2026 revision of the instruction (none found).

## 5. Storage costs

### Takeaway
Bank lockers cost about Rs 1,000-20,000 a year plus 18% GST. Home insurance not researched.

### Cited Findings
- SBI: small Rs 1,500-2,000 (metro/urban), medium Rs 3,000-4,000, large Rs 5,000-6,000; lower in semi-urban/rural — [BankBazaar/CreditMantri summary](https://www.creditmantri.com/sbi-locker-charges/).
- HDFC: new "Metro Plus" category from 1 April 2026; small Rs 2,000-7,500, medium Rs 4,000-12,500, large Rs 6,000-20,000, plus 18% GST — [ClearTax](https://cleartax.in/s/hdfc-bank-locker-charges).

### Inferences
- A small SBI locker (about Rs 2,000 plus GST, about Rs 2,360) on Rs 1.6 lakh of gold costs about 1.5%/yr; storage is material for small holdings.

### Gaps
- Home/jewellery insurance premiums; Fetched pages not opened directly.

## 6. Worked example (my own arithmetic, assumptions stated)

Assumptions: 10 g 24k at spot Rs 15,700/g (illustrative; a recent price quoted by a deals account), so spot value Rs 1,57,000. Retail premium 3%, GST 3% on (metal + premium), sale after 3 years (more than 24 months, so LTCG 12.5% + 4% cess; surcharge ignored), spot rises 10%, buyback deduction 3% below spot.

- Purchase: 1,57,000 + premium 4,710 = 1,61,710; GST 4,851; total paid Rs 1,66,561 (about 6.1% over spot).
- Sale: spot 1,72,700; less 3% deduction (5,181) = Rs 1,67,519.
- Gain: 1,67,519 - 1,66,561 = Rs 958; LTCG tax 12.5% = Rs 120 (+4% cess = Rs 125). Net proceeds Rs 1,67,394; net profit Rs 833 (0.5% over 3 years) against a 10% rise in spot.
- Break-even spot rise (pre-tax, where tax is nil): 1.0609 / (1 - deduction) - 1 = 7.2% (1% deduction), 9.4% (3%), 12.9% (6%). At 9.4% over 3 years that is about 3.0% a year.
- If you buy with a 0% premium and still pay GST, the break-even at a 3% deduction is 6.2%. For 22k, GST is the same; add making charges (often 3-10%+) to the cost with likely no making-charge recovery on exit, which pushes break-even higher. Not sourced.

### Gaps
- Actual spot/IBJA rates on the date, premium levels, and spread data not retrieved; the 3% premium is an assumption.
